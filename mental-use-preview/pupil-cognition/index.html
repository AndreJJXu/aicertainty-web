Mental rotation · question → evidence → use

Does a larger pupil change always mean better cognitive performance?

Read the finding first. Then check what it can be used for.

01

Current answer

Original article ↗

Not necessarily. A larger change can accompany slower, less accurate responses.

This example concerns a mental-rotation task. Its condition contrasts do not turn pupil change into a personal ability score or directly test working-memory capacity.

Sample & measurement limits

Pupil change is trial maximum minus the baseline from about the first 40 ms after stimulus onset. Carry-over from preceding trials limits interpretation.

  • Only non-mirrored pairs enter these analyses; angle-specific reaction-time outliers are excluded.

  • Task-condition effects are not between-person pupil–performance correlations, working-memory-capacity measurements, or pupil interventions.
