What we mean by human state

A signal is not a human state.

Skin conductance is not stress; a paused cursor is not hesitation. The state is an inference built on the signal, and the inference is where the claims — and the responsibility — begin. This page fixes the terms the rest of the site uses.

The six principles

P1

A signal is not a human state.

A measurement is an input, never an answer. Skin conductance, eye movement, voice pitch, choice latency: these are signals, and the human state is an inference built on top of them. Copy that lets a signal masquerade as a state is wrong before it is inaccurate.

In practice: every human-state term on this site resolves to a construct, a signal, and a stated inference boundary — or it is not used as a state.

P2

Uncertainty is data.

A confidence band, a contested link, an honest null — these are results, not embarrassments. A system that hides its uncertainty is asking to be trusted rather than checked.

In practice: uncertainty renders in the same visual weight as measurement, and null results print at full size — the program's own NONE_CONFIRMED among them.

P3

Certainty about evidence, not certainty about the mind.

We do not promise to know what someone feels. We promise you can check what a claim about them rests on: the construct, the signal, the evidence, the boundary. That is the only certainty on offer, and it is the one that survives scrutiny.

In practice: the word “certainty” appears on this site only as the name, or inside this sentence.

P4

Human state deserves first-class standing.

Inferences about people enter intelligent systems as inputs, whether or not anyone declared them. First-class standing means declared constructs, named signals, stated evidence, explicit boundaries — not unlabeled side effects of a model.

In practice: any human-state inference discussed on this site is discussed with its declaration attached.

P5

The loop is the unit of work: Sense → Model → Calibrate → Adapt → Verify.

Measurement is not done when the number appears. It is done when the system's response to the number has been verified. Adaptation without verification is drift.

In practice: the five stages appear as one unbroken loop, never as a feature list.

P6

Claim boundaries are part of the contribution.

A result without its edge is an overclaim in waiting. Stating what a work does not show is not a disclaimer appended at the end; it is part of the result.

In practice: every work mentioned on this site ships with its boundary — “✓ Established / ✗ Explicitly not claimed” — at equal visual weight to its findings.

How claims are labeled here

Every empirical sentence on this site carries exactly one of five tier labels, adjacent to the claim:

  • Established background

    settled science, independent of us. Context, never proof.

  • Supported company research

    findings from this program that passed review or are published with stated evidence.

  • Current work

    the live build and active manuscripts, with explicit status. A status is not a result.

  • Hypothesis

    a testable expectation, stated with the test that could falsify it.

  • Long-term vision

    direction we choose to publish. Aspiration; implies no existing capability.

Labels are never upgraded because a sentence sounds better. If a sentence only works one tier up, the sentence goes.
