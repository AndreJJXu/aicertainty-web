AI Certainty — certainty about evidence, not certainty about the mind.

Human state is always inferred. We are building the layer that makes the inference checkable.

AI Certainty studies and builds the measurement layer between humans and intelligent systems.

Intelligent systems increasingly act on inferences about human states — attention, affect, intent, trust — while the evidence behind those inferences stays invisible. We make that evidence explicit and checkable: named constructs, measured signals, stated evidence, declared inference boundaries, linked in one versioned artifact.

Every intelligent system holds beliefs about human states. Almost none can show their evidence. We think that is a measurement problem — and measurement problems have boundaries.

The asymmetry is the problem: a system can act on an inference about you, and you cannot inspect what that inference rests on. Closing that gap is not a feature. It is infrastructure.

What we mean by human state — the stance document →

Make human state a first-class input to AI.

First-class means a human-state inference enters a system the way any serious input does: declared, sourced, and bounded. A signal is not a human state — the state is an inference built on the signal, and the inference is where the claims live. One loop instruments the whole path:

  • Sense

    Name the signal and the instrument that produced it.

  • Model

    State the construct the signal is supposed to index.

  • Calibrate

    Anchor the reading in population, context, and uncertainty.

  • Adapt

    Let the system respond only inside the declared boundary.

  • Verify

    Check that the response holds. Adaptation without verification is drift.

The Human State Evidence Graph

One versioned artifact linking five object types — construct, signal, measurement, evidence, inference boundary — so that every claim about a person carries its provenance and its edge. Versioned like software.

What the graph is, and is not →

Three questions, three real records. Statuses verbatim; boundaries attached.

Measure Preprint · arXiv 2025

What can a signal actually tell you?

Chain-of-Affective — across eight LLM families, the work reports stable, family-specific affective fingerprints under repeated negative exposure.

What it contributes

A measurable form of affective state in model behavior — where the measurement target is the model's output, not a person's self-report.

Ground Under review · AAAI 2027 — a status, not a result

What would change that conclusion?

Evidence Before Severity — 742 dialogues · 21,628 evaluations · an 869,268-record evidence atlas from 29 sources, with a 17,280-call robustness extension built to ask exactly this question. The audit reports safety judgments shifting with severity cues rather than expressed evidence.

What it contributes

A same-text method for separating expressed evidence from severity cues in LLM safety judgment.

Align Published · IPM 2026

What is the system not allowed to infer?

MARS — audio-to-image generation; reported +28.73% semantic relevance over the best audio-to-image baseline on VGGSound. Its declared boundary: “therapeutic or accessibility applications are motivation, not validated outcomes.”

What it contributes

Cross-modal alignment published with its claim boundary attached — the house style, on its first paper.

A doctrine behind the discipline

Evidence before confidence. — No number without provenance; no claim without its boundary.

A signal is not a human state. — The state is an inference, and the inference is where responsibility begins.

Uncertainty is data. — A confidence band, a contested link, an honest null: results, not embarrassments.
