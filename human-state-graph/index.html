Human State Evidence Graph

A versioned, machine-readable evidence graph that answers one question: given observable human signals, what inference about a human state is scientifically justified, under what conditions, and with what uncertainty? Five object types — construct, signal, measurement, evidence, inference boundary — linked so every claim carries its provenance, and every boundary is explicit.

What it is

  • ✓

    An evidence graph whose primary unit is the claim, not the paper. Studies attach to claims as evidence records, each with provenance back to its source.

  • ✓

    Versioned like software: the schema, the corpus, and the confidence rubric each carry a version.

  • ✓

    Contradictions and null results as first-class data — preserved, never averaged away.

  • ✓

    An auditable confidence rubric: labels from INSUFFICIENT to VERY_HIGH, plus CONFLICTED, computed by stated rules rather than judgment calls.

  • ✓

    “Insufficient evidence” is a normal output, printed at full size.

What it is not

  • ✗

    It never returns a verdict about a person. It returns the status of the evidence.

  • ✗

    Not a diagnostic instrument. Population-level associations are not individual-level results.

  • ✗

    Not a deployed product: no users, no integrations, no uptime.

  • ✗

    Not finished. v0.1 is in progress, and nothing in it is a stable reference yet.

  • ✗

    Not a substitute for the literature. Every record points back to its source.

CONCEPT · v0.1 DIRECTION

The query, walked through

Take one concrete question — the kind the graph exists to answer — and walk it through the five object types. This walkthrough is illustrative: a concept of the intended behavior, not a live query. The records behind it are v0.1, in progress.

  1. Step 1 · Signal

    The observation enters as a signal: pupil diameter (SIG_PUPIL), in the eye-tracking modality (MOD_EYE). The graph separates the feature — task-evoked pupillary dilation (FEAT_PUP_DIL) — from baseline diameter, because a baseline and an evoked response are different measurements.

  2. Step 2 · Construct

    The feature points at a construct — cognitive load (CON0030) — over the edge operationalized_by. The construct, not the signal, is where the claim lives. The ontology keeps cognitive load distinct from task difficulty: one is a state of the person, the other a property of the task.

  3. Step 3 · Evidence

    The edge carries claim clusters, and each cluster carries evidence records: one study's contribution, with provenance back to its source, its population, its task, and its sample. Where studies are few and small, the rubric returns INSUFFICIENT — not as a failure, but as the answer.

  4. Step 4 · Confounders

    The same dilation answers to other constructs. Arousal moves it; surprise moves it; screen luminance moves it before anything else. Each confounder is a node in the graph, typed confounded_by — not a footnote.

  5. Step 5 · Boundary

    The construct carries an inference-boundary card: what may be inferred (group-level, task-specific, with uncertainty attached) and what may not (a verdict about an individual; a causal claim from correlational designs; anything covert). The pupil is not a lie detector.

Return

Group-level support here is thin, the signal's specificity is documented as limited, and the boundary travels with the answer.

Input modalities

The modality layer of the ontology — the signal families the graph can carry evidence about:

  • Eye tracking and pupillometry MOD_EYE
  • EEG MOD_EEG
  • Behavior and interaction logs MOD_BEHAVIOR
  • Peripheral physiology and wearables — heart rate, HRV, EDA, PPG MOD_PHYSIO · MOD_PPG
  • Self-report MOD_SELFR
  • fMRI MOD_FMRI
  • Audio MOD_AUDIO
  • Video MOD_VIDEO

Initial domains under exploration

Nine state families form the v0.1 scope — listed as initial domains under exploration, not as validated coverage:

  1. 1 Cognitive load and mental workload
  2. 2 Sustained attention and vigilance
  3. 3 Mental fatigue
  4. 4 Sleepiness
  5. 5 Stress
  6. 6 Affect — valence and arousal
  7. 7 Trust
  8. 8 Motivation and reward states
  9. 9 Mind wandering and task engagement

Current work
The ontology also carries adjacent, more sensitive constructs. Their boundaries are deliberately conservative, and some states are excluded from inference entirely at v0.1.

Status

Current work
Status: v0.1, in progress — Rev. 2026-09. The schema is settling; claim clusters and inference-boundary cards are being assembled for the initial domains. Under this site's claims vocabulary, everything on this page is CURRENT WORK: a status is not a result.

What v0.1 will ship

  • A frozen schema, documented in the open, with every node type and edge predicate defined.

  • Claim clusters for the initial domains, each carrying its evidence records, its provenance, and its rubric label.

  • An inference-boundary card per construct: what may, and may not, be inferred.

  • A public changelog — the graph versions like software, and the changelog is where the versions live.

  • Contradictions and null results printed at the same size as positive findings.

No dates are attached. The roadmap is direction, not schedule.
