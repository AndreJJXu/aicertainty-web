YOUR QUESTION

Does a larger pupil mean better working memory?

01

Current answer

[1][2][3][4]

Larger is not a general rule for better memory.

Four papers examine different pupil measures and tasks. Resting size, task-evoked dilation, overload and remembered brightness must be read separately; associations in one condition do not supply a personal score or causal benefit.

Resting size

A resting baseline did not track working-memory capacity; a reliable baseline is not automatically predictive.

[2][4]

Task-evoked response

Positive associations occur in selected loads, trials and sessions; the measurement window matters.

[3][4]

Overload

More items can lead to a plateau and decline, rather than continued dilation.

[3]

Remembered content

In the PNAS task, dark and bright memory items reverse the direction of association.

Supporting evidence · conditional[1]

A conditional link with memory precision

Across the three experiments in this paper, better recall precision was associated with larger pupils for dark memory items, but smaller pupils for bright items.

Analysis sample
68 participants · Healthy young volunteers; three distinct experiment samples
Design
Within-person correlations compared by brightness, with experiment as a between-person factor

These are within-person associations. They do not show that enlarging a pupil improves memory, or provide an individual prediction threshold.

Qualifying evidence · scope matters[1]

A pupil difference is not a demonstrated memory gain

In Experiment 2, dark versus bright cued memory items differed in mean pupil size. The brightness main effect on recall precision was not detected in the same 23 participants.

Analysis sample
23 participants · Healthy volunteers aged 21–29 in Experiment 2
Design

The two tests address different outcomes. Non-detection is not proof of no effect or equivalence, and the cue-validity advantage is a separate factor.

In 102 participants, resting pupil size was not associated with letter–number sequencing performance (r = 0.05, p = 0.65). BF01 = 7.26 favored the null under the authors’ model.

      02

      Direct opposition to the brightness mechanism

      Not found

      The added papers limit a universal “larger is better” rule through different measures and tasks. They do not directly replicate or refute the PNAS remembered-brightness mechanism. No direct opposing replication of that mechanism is included; this is a targeted selection, not a systematic review.

      Null or non-monotonic findings in a different measure limit generalization; they are not opposing replications of the brightness mechanism. Non-detection does not establish equivalence.

      03

      When this answer applies

      Which measure?

      Resting size, pre-trial baseline, baseline variability and evoked change are different measurements.

      Which task and sample?

      Digit recall, n-back and visual-memory precision differ. Endpoint samples and repeated sessions cannot be pooled as independent people.

      What cannot be inferred?

      No clinical prediction, universal memory score or causal improvement follows. Four publications are not four replications of one mechanism.
