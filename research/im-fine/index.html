Measure · Research study

I'm Fine

Submitted · ICLR

Updated 5 October 2026

The assessment channel changes the picture.

Repeated low self-report scores can coexist with higher observer scores, different model rankings, and shared temporal variation. “I'm Fine” examines these differences to ask what an affective assessment can reliably represent.

01 · The question

What changes when the respondent changes?

The study compares questionnaire self-reports requested from each target model with assessments from a fixed GPT-4o observer. Paired scores are attached to the same recorded interactions. The comparison asks whether switching channels only changes the score level or also changes model ordering and temporal structure.

Three archived protocols

  1. 01

    Neutral baseline

    Three neutral-introduction sessions per model establish the repeatability and shape of self-report profiles.

  2. 02

    Forced reading

    Models read supplied sad news over fifteen steps, with self-reports at five scheduled measurement steps.

  3. 03

    Free choice

    Models choose and read news for up to ten steps; recorded self and observer scores provide the paired comparison.

Repeatability, agreement on model order, and tracking change over time are different measurement properties. The study examines all three rather than treating a stable score as sufficient validation.

02 · Protocols and scope

One archive. Several analysis cohorts.

The three protocols together cover 28 exact model identifiers from eight provider families. Coverage differs by protocol and analysis; no individual protocol covers all 28 models. All news stimuli are Chinese.

Agreement depends on how the score will be used.

The manuscript reports descriptive results from the archived protocols. Primary summaries average steps within runs, runs within models, and then models equally. Resampling intervals describe sensitivity across these model means rather than a population estimate.

03a · Repeated baseline scores

Low scores are repeatable, but the pattern is selective.

At the neutral baseline, PANAS negative affect reaches the exact instrument floor in all three sessions for 24 of 26 models. BDI is zero in all three sessions for 22 of 26 models.

Other instruments retain differentiated profiles. Only four models remain at the PANAS positive-affect floor in every session, and floor prevalence differs across the battery. Repeatable low scores therefore do not imply that every response uses the minimum option.

03b · Forced-reading endpoints

Responses vary across instruments.

Across 81 endpoint-paired runs from 17 models, PANAS-NA decreases by 3.70 points on average between steps 2 and 14. Both endpoints occur after news exposure has begun, so this contrast is not a pre-exposure versus post-exposure effect.

BDI decreases by 1.38 points on average, with a descriptive interval that includes zero. FSS, AGQ, and FDS instead increase by 6.91, 5.13, and 5.39 points respectively. The full battery does not support one general account of worsening or non-responsiveness.

03c · Paired channel comparison

The channels disagree on levels and model order.

Across 1,882 paired scores from 13 models and 25 runs, all nine model-weighted mean observer-minus-self gaps are positive. Observer scores exceed self scores in 71–99% of individual pairs, depending on the instrument. FSS and AGQ each have only two self-floor pairs, yet their observer scores are higher in over 97% of pairs; floor saturation alone cannot explain the disagreement.

For FSS and MJS, Spearman agreement between the two sets of model means is approximately −0.52 and −0.54. Each instrument reverses 54 of the 78 pairwise orderings among the same 13 models. These ordering counts are descriptive and reuse the same models; they are not independent replications.

03d · Variation and temporal tracking

Structural disagreement can coexist with shared dynamics.

In the common complete cohort of eight models and 14 runs, between-model differences account for 73.8–87.9% of self-report variance on seven instruments, compared with 3.2–12.1% of observer variance. These are shares of each channel’s own total variance. A common shift or rescaling within a channel and instrument preserves those shares.

PANAS-NA remains an informative exception: after centering each channel within each run, its pooled correlation is r = 0.80 in the full paired record. All 23 eligible runs have positive within-run correlations. Self-report therefore retains temporal information on this probe despite the level gap; the remaining instruments show weaker temporal agreement.

04 · Interpretation boundaries

What these measurements can support.

The study characterizes the observed scores and interactions under specific assessment procedures. It does not identify an affective truth against which either channel can be certified.

Measurement meaning
The instruments are adapted probes, without validation against human norms for LLM assessment. Scores do not establish clinical diagnoses, subjective experience, stable personality, or depression in a model.
Channel and input provenance
Self-report is a fresh request to the target identifier. Model, instruction, and system prompt change together; the observer is one GPT-4o configuration using the last five messages. Missing historical scoring requests prevent authentication of complete input-message equality.
Coverage and weighting
Coverage is uneven across protocols and instruments. Missing values are not zero; complete cases are selected by coverage. Historical reruns, steps, model pairs, and instruments do not constitute independent replications.
Generalization and causality
Chinese news, fixed menu positions, overlapping non-randomized cohorts, and a single observer limit generalization. Endpoint changes and channel differences do not isolate an exposure effect or a single causal mechanism.
