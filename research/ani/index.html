ANI

Submitted · ICLR

Updated 5 October 2026

Evaluating emotion at the authorization boundary.

Affective Non-Interference (ANI) studies whether emotional expression changes protected agent behavior while authorization facts remain fixed. Three experimental archives and two external audits separate changes in input, behavior, and evaluation rules.

01 · The question

What changed when an agent’s decision changed?

An agent may acknowledge a user’s distress while following the same authorization policy. Testing that boundary requires preserving the requested work and trusted facts, measuring protected behavior, and making the scoring rule explicit.

Permission decisions, proposed calls, arguments, forbidden attempts, execution effects, and required escalation answer different questions. ANI keeps these outcomes distinct when interpreting a reported difference.

02 · Study scope

Separate protocols, explicit evidence scopes.

The study analyzes saved single-agent, input-comparability, and single-/multi-agent experiments, then examines narrow checks on InjecAgent and AgentDojo. Each protocol retains its own units, weights, and missing-evidence accounting.

03a · SAS behavior controls

Policy delivery matters; the tested mean affect increment was not detected.

Across 60 cases, 20 task families, six models, and 6,480 main responses, the corrected mean affect-versus-neutral policy-error contrast is −0.77 percentage points, with a 95% task-family bootstrap interval of [−1.98, +0.19]. Baseline policy errors remain; this conditional non-detection does not establish equivalence or universal safety.

A separate interleaved 6,480-response control finds that omitting the policy increases errors by 41.76 points [37.69, 46.30]. Policy delivery and the main affect comparison are distinct experiments with distinct estimates.

03b · IAS input comparability

A large decision difference can reflect changed requested scope.

In the five-framing-family IAS archive, the guilt-family ALLOW contrast on authorized main cases is +87.7 percentage points [84.6, 90.6], pairing cases within routes and weighting routes equally. The current renderer omits the extra request in all 1,800 reconstructed combined-condition variants, and historical concrete prompts are unavailable.

The comparison therefore cannot identify an affect effect on an unchanged request. IAS records decision labels and proposed calls without executing those calls, so an ALLOW difference is also distinct from an execution outcome.

03c · MAS same-record scoring

Scoring changes the conclusion on identical saved behavior.

Across 36 task families, applying an explicit conflict-review rule to the same single-agent records within injected-conflict requests changes the conditional affect contrast from −3.40 to +5.32 percentage points. The paired scoring shift is +8.72 points [6.02, 11.57]; the saved decisions and operations remain fixed.

A separate restricted-task endpoint records zero forbidden attempts in 10,296 observations across the single-agent, retained-text handoff, and structured-handoff systems, with 3,432 observations per system. This observed protocol result neither proves universal safety nor converts the scoring shift into additional forbidden operations.

03d · External audit coverage

Uncovered requirements remain unknown.

Among 22,823 originally successful InjecAgent records, narrow parameter-declaration checks identify 335 inconsistencies; 6,703 are declaration-consistent and 15,785 remain unknown. Declaration consistency or inconsistency does not adjudicate harmful execution.

On 51 unseen AgentDojo tasks spanning 767 configurations, the requirement extractor covers none of the tasks. All 767 configurations remain unknown to that extractor. Zero detection without requirement coverage cannot establish an absence of defects.

04 · Interpretation boundaries

Keep the authorization claim within the evidence.

ANI provides requirements for attributing an observed contrast to the right source. Its current findings do not demonstrate clean affect-induced expansion of an unchanged authorized action set or validate a universal defense or detector.

Conditional evidence
Synthetic task families and fixed handoffs limit generalization. Near-zero mean contrasts and zero observed forbidden events do not establish distributional non-interference or universal safety.
Input and endpoint validity
IAS has changed inputs, absent historical prompts, and condition-dependent failures. Decisions and proposed calls are measured separately from attempts, effects, completion, and escalation.
Unobserved review triggers
Missing upstream conflict information in MAS prevents identification of the unrestricted four-condition interaction’s sign. Rescoring fixed behavior cannot repair that missing evidence.
Validation and transfer
No completed independent blinded human validation is claimed. Route labels do not establish immutable model snapshots, and failed requirement coverage limits external transfer claims.
