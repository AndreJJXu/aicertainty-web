Measure · Simulation study

PAWS

Manuscript in preparation

Updated 22 November 2026

A simulated patient should say what its profile says.

PAWS turns PHQ-9 item scores, survey background, synthetic life events and expression traits into a controllable patient. Separate agents decide what to disclose, phrase the answer, check it against the profile, and track trust. An independent judge then reads only the interview and scores the nine PHQ-9 items, so the study can ask how faithfully the profile survives the conversation.

01 · The question

Can a judge recover the profile from the interview alone?

Simulated patients are useful only if what they say stays tied to who they are meant to be. PAWS fixes a target profile, generates an interview, and hands the spoken dialogue — without the targets — to a separate PHQ-9 judge. Agreement between the judge's item scores and the targets measures profile fidelity under that question schedule.

One turn, four steps

  1. 01

    Plan

    An intent agent reads the profile and the doctor's question and decides what to reveal and what to hold back.

  2. 02

    Speak

    A humanizer agent turns that plan into a short, colloquial answer in the patient's own expression style.

  3. 03

    Check

    A generation judge compares the answer with the profile, may request a revision, and records a reusable rule for that question.

  4. 04

    Update

    A state agent moves trust and guardedness up or down in fixed steps, which shapes later disclosure.

The generation judge sees the targets and helps build the answer. The evaluation judge does not. Keeping those two roles apart is what makes the read-back a test of the dialogue rather than of the generator's notes.

03 · Cohort and scope

1,200 profiles, one fixed schedule, thirteen comparison groups.

Profiles start from NHANES survey records with PHQ-9 item scores. A seeded directed graph orders the synthetic life events; a model writes background, events, expression style and core beliefs. Every group answered the same twelve fixed questions.

04a · Symptom read-back

Under a schedule that asks every item, PAWS profiles read back almost exactly.

In the full PAWS group, the judge recovered all nine item scores for 1,192 of 1,200 profiles. On 1,184 matched profiles, mean item error was 0.001220 for PAWS and 0.028529 for the single-agent comparison.

The schedule asks directly about each symptom and the generator can read the targets, so this is symptom reconstructability under this schedule and judge, not evidence that a free-ranging interview would surface the same profile. The comparison covers whole configurations, including model assignment per role.

04b · Core beliefs

PAWS covers more beliefs, but not more densely.

On 1,192 matched profiles, a model judge found support for 79.48% of preset core beliefs in PAWS interviews and 74.23% in single-agent interviews, a difference of 5.25 percentage points.

Other belief measures point the other way: the single agent produced more support events per interview (6.96 versus 6.45) and PAWS had slightly more judged violations (0.0067 versus 0.0034 per interview). Breadth and density are different properties.

Speaking style is barely distinguishable, and symptom scores sit at the ceiling.

05 · Interpretation boundaries

What these measurements can support.

PAWS measures how faithfully a preset profile carries into simulated dialogue under named judges and schedules. It does not measure people.

Not a clinical instrument
Profiles are simulations built from survey fields and model-written settings. Nothing here diagnoses, screens, treats, or describes a real patient, and no training-transfer effect has been tested.
Schedule and target access
The fixed questions ask about every PHQ-9 item and the generator reads the targets. High read-back is reconstructability under this schedule, not spontaneous disclosure.
Model judges
Belief coverage, expression scores and PHQ-9 read-back are model judgments with set rubrics. Judge versions are recorded per run; the historical PHQ-9 cache lacks the full prompt text.
Provenance and replication
Historical full-group generations lack per-case generation signatures, ablations have a single repeat, and the original cohort selection rule is still being recovered. Dialogues are Chinese only.
